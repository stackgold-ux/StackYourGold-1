# Why Are Central Banks Buying Gold at the Fastest Rate in History?

## The Headline Number

In 2024, the world's central banks added **1,045 tonnes of gold** to their reserves, according to the World Gold Council.

That capped a streak with no modern precedent: central bank gold demand topped **1,000 tonnes for the third consecutive year** — a "hat-trick," as the World Gold Council put it.

For context: in the decades before 2010, central banks were often *net sellers* of gold, slowly trimming their holdings. Today they are, collectively, the most aggressive buyers on the planet — more than jewelry demand, more than tech, more than private investors.

These are the same institutions that print the money you use every day. The same ones that assure you the dollar is strong and inflation is under control. And yet, when nobody is watching the vault, they are converting fiat into metal as fast as their balance sheets will allow.

**What do they know?**

---

## The Buyers: Who's Leading the Rush

Not every country is buying. But the ones that are tell a specific story.

### 🇵🇱 Poland — The NATO Frontier

Poland has been one of Europe's most aggressive buyers, publicly stating a goal of holding 20% of its reserves in gold. For a frontline NATO state on Russia's border, the logic is blunt: gold is an asset no foreign power can freeze, seize, or devalue by decree.

### 🇭🇺 Hungary — The Record Setter

Hungary's central bank raised its gold reserves to a **record 110 tonnes**, framing the move explicitly as a hedge against geopolitical and monetary risk.

### 🇮🇳 India — The Repatriator

The Reserve Bank of India made headlines in 2024 when it **repatriated 100 tonnes of gold from the United Kingdom** back to domestic vaults. The message was unmistakable: gold held overseas is gold you don't fully control.

### 🇹🇷 Turkey, 🇨🇳 China, 🇨🇿 Czech Republic, 🇰🇿 Kazakhstan…

Smaller economies, same pattern. They're accumulating — and, notably, few are selling.

## Why Now? Three Forces Driving the Rush

### 1. The Weaponization of the Dollar

In 2022, the U.S. and its allies froze roughly **$300 billion** in Russian central bank reserves — money Russia held in dollars and euros, parked in Western financial institutions. Overnight, it became untouchable.

Every central bank governor on earth watched that happen.

The lesson was impossible to miss: **your dollar-denominated reserves are only truly yours as long as Washington says so.**

Gold held in your own vault has no counterparty. No SWIFT message can freeze it. No executive order can seize it. For any country with a tense relationship with the West — China, Russia, Turkey, Iran — that reality reshaped reserve strategy overnight.

### 2. The Push to Bypass the Dollar

The BRICS bloc (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa) plus new members like Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Iran, and Egypt now represents a huge share of global GDP and population. Those governments have openly discussed trade currencies that reduce reliance on the dollar — and preparation for that shift, in practice, means accumulating gold.

### 3. The Debt Math Doesn't Close

U.S. national debt has blown past **$35 trillion** and keeps climbing, with interest payments now rivaling major federal spending categories. Central bankers aren't naive — they can read the projection lines. A currency backed by trillions in obligations with no credible path to balance is, over the long run, a short.

Gold doesn't need a bailout. Gold doesn't need to raise its debt ceiling. Gold just *is.*
